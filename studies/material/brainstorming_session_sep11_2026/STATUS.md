the value of the document is that it records what killed each one.

### The pattern

Thirty candidates, and one number decides most of them: the round-trip fee. **Seventeen directional / microstructure / flow studies produced zero accessible edges** — the best measured is 2.5bp against a 5-10bp taker or 4bp maker cost. Everything still alive is either payment for *providing* (funding carry +3.4%/yr untimed, +7.7% timed; the volatility premium behind the gated butterfly) or a filter that deletes false positives (overlap 3.7-7.4×, placebo, drift, unblocked). The newest result fits the pattern rather than breaking it: the covered call's arithmetic alpha is t = +1.28 to +1.62, its geometric edge survives Bonferroni only at p ≈ 0.08, and put-call parity shows the "new" leg decomposition is BTC's +0.430%/wk drift restated.

## Indicator inventory

Every distinct measurable quantity this programme has built, wired up, or validated — whether or not it produced a tradeable edge. Most entries are dead as standalone signals. That is not the same as useless: a series that cannot time an entry may still size a position, veto one, or tell you what you are looking at. The `Usable as` column is the operative field.

Scale used throughout: `standalone signal` (can open a position on its own) / `filter or veto` (can suppress or gate another signal) / `risk input` (sizing, stops, exposure, expectations) / `context only` (descriptive; do not act on it) / `nothing` (measured, empty, and reproducing it is a waste of compute).

Round-trip cost reference for every "vs fee" comparison below: 10bp taker–taker, 7bp maker–taker, 4bp maker–maker on Binance USDT-M futures (maker is 0.02%/side — it is not free). BTC daily sigma ≈ 2.355% at ~45% annualised vol.

### The reusable pieces

Eight components survived the programme and are worth building on. Everything else in this document is either context, a guard rail, or a closed question.

1. **Funding-carry timing by current-print quartile** — +0.75 lag-1 autocorrelation turns the current print into a forecast of the next; holding only the top half yields **+7.7%/yr vs +3.4% untimed** and skips the 20% of 30-day windows that are negative. Edge/noise **0.780**, **n = 7 trades to reach t=2** — three times the ratio of anything else measured. Monthly rebalance (~12 trades/yr) is the right frequency: the 0.20% two-leg round trip costs 21 days of carry.
2. **The IV−RV spread, sampled non-overlapping** — **+3.27 vol pts, t=+2.15, positive in 72% of 57 weeks**, no significant decay (t=−1.26; trend slope t=−1.50). The IV/RV *ratio* sits at 92% of its 2021 value while the IV *level* halved: dormant, not competed away, and it scales back up with vol.
3. **The viability gate, `margin = hist_win − need_win ≥ +5pp`** — the executable form of #2, priced off live bid/ask (bid for legs sold, ask for legs bought) against ≥1000 days of |H-day move| history. It is honest enough to say **NO-GO on all four candidates at −3.9 / −1.0 / −4.3 / −28.6pp** on 2026-09-09, which is exactly what a gate is for. Pair it with the 43% wing cost and the **0.6–0.7 defined-risk haircut**.
4. **The OI-drop cascade proxy** (`|z|≥4` 24h return **and** OI drop ≥0.30%) — **40× concentration in the top 1% of liquidation bars, z=+69.9**, validated against 53,398 real liquidations. It matters more now than when it was built: the raw liquidation feed died 2024-10-14, and this proxy is the only way to keep measuring forced flow, off 5m OI that reaches back to 2021.
5. **The liquidation-density map and the 24h extreme, as risk inputs** — density **1.21×** monotonic across quintiles (z=+9.1) surviving the visit-count control at every stratum; the 24h rolling low is the nearest level on **27.0% of cascades vs 8.2% of random (3.31×)**. It predicts **initiation only** — every attempt to use it as a target or range boundary failed (corr(distance, travel) = −0.043). Use it to displace stops and to size, never to aim.
6. **The EMA150 gate plus the EMA(20)/EMA(100) consensus veto on S-003** — Sharpe **1.00 → 1.07**, maxDD **−59% → −41%**, and the veto binds on only **~1% of days**. Credible because of the plateau: five EMA pairs all land Sharpe 1.04–1.08. Ship it only after fixing the two live defects (no stop on the first day of a new position; `cur_dir` not reconciled after a stop fill).
7. **The two-axis screen and the learnability formula** — `n for t=2 = (2·noise/edge)²`; reject if ratio < 0.05 **and** < 1000 trades/yr, or if edge < 2× round-trip fee. Applied retrospectively it rejects **six of ten prior studies before any compute**. Its companion constant is the sigma-capture ceiling: prediction captures **3–13% of horizon sigma (clustering near 5%)** while risk premia capture **20–80%**, which sets the **minimum viable hold at 2.9 days taker / 11.1h maker-maker**.
8. **The methodology corrections** — overlap inflation (**3.7× on 7-day windows sampled daily, 7.4× on first-passage labels**), the unblocked-variant rule for position-state simulators (caught a manufactured **+0.38pp**), the placebo/publication-lag rule (killed t=+3.2 order-arrival and +0.45 ETF correlation), the random-level fee floor (**−0.0388%**), the drift control, and the causal 60/40 chronological split. These are why the programme's negatives can be trusted, and they are the cheapest thing here to reuse.

## How these findings could be used

The programme's central result is a ranking, not a strategy: price **prediction** on BTC captures 3–13% of available sigma (clustering near 5%), while **risk premia** capture 20–80%. Seventeen prediction studies produced zero accessible edges. Everything below therefore starts from the same question — *what is this component being paid for?* — and treats "generates a signal" as the weakest possible reason to keep something.

A second organising fact governs which archetype is worth building first: **how long it takes to know whether it works.** The learnability formula `n for t=2 = (2·noise/edge)²` (RATIO_FINDINGS.md) puts the candidates in an order that has nothing to do with how interesting they are:

| component | edge/noise ratio | n for t=2 | trades/yr | time to validate |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| funding carry, 7d hold | +0.780 | 7 | 52 | 0.1 yr |
| funding carry, 1d hold | +0.717 | 8 | 365 | 0.0 yr |
| short straddle 7d | +0.231 | 75 | 52 | 1.4 yr |
| short straddle 30d | +0.080 | 623 | 12 | 51.9 yr |
| buy & hold 90d | +0.339 | 35 | 4 | 8.7 yr |
| 1h contrarian, 2h hold | +0.026 | 6,092 | 4,380 | 1.4 yr |
| RANDOM 4h null | +0.004 | 204,793 | 2,190 | 93.5 yr |

Anything whose "time to validate" exceeds the horizon over which the market itself is stable is not a strategy, it is a belief. That is the frame for the six archetypes.

### 1. Exposure management — signals that cannot predict but reduce time-in-market

**Mechanism.** If a signal has zero directional alpha but exits during periods whose *realised variance* is above average, total return is unchanged while volatility drag falls. Because wealth compounds geometrically, cutting weekly sd raises CAGR even when mean arithmetic return falls. This is the only archetype where a signal with a genuinely zero mean can still be worth running.

**Components that feed it.**
- **%R swing exposure result** (WILLIAMS_FINDINGS.md §4): time in market **45.8%** for **+279%** total vs buy & hold **+246.7%** over ~1500 days, n=264 trades.
- **The covered-call arithmetic/geometric gap** (new result): paired arithmetic difference vs spot **t = +1.28 / +1.54 / +1.62 / +0.84** across ATM/+2/+5/+10% — none reaches 2 — while the paired **log-return** difference reaches **t = +2.03 / +2.32 / +2.41 / +1.56**. The gap *is* the mechanism: variance reduction converting into geometric return.
- **EMA-cross 20/100 consensus veto** on S-003 (adx_emax_unified_v1.pine): maxDD **−59% → −41%**, Sharpe 1.00 → 1.07, while binding on only **~1% of days**.
- **Rescue scheme "C → cut + 72h cooldown"** (WILLIAMS §6): maxDD **−10.2%** vs base **−23.2%**, at a per-trade return of +0.072% (t=+0.70) vs base +0.238% (t=+1.65) — return given up, drawdown more than halved.

**Evidenced vs hypothesis.** *Partially evidenced, with a specific and serious hole.* The drawdown reductions are real and measured. The **return-neutrality is not established**: the %R swing's per-trade edge fails train/test (**TRAIN +0.458%, t=+2.36 → TEST −0.090%, t=−0.43**), the entry signal alone is **−0.197% (t=−0.87)**, and 2023 alone (+0.617%, t=+2.74) carries the whole thing. So "equal return at half exposure" is one sampled path, not a demonstrated property. On the covered call, the adversarial review's studentized bootstrap on a sharply left-skewed sample (skew −1.98 / −2.46 / −3.20) roughly halves the naive p-values to **0.031 / 0.021 / 0.021**, ≈ **0.08 after Bonferroni over the four strikes** — the geometric edge is suggestive, not established.

**Cheapest test.** Re-run the %R swing exposure claim through the **start-date sensitivity grid** that already exists for the covered call (start 2021…2026, cached `spot_BTCUSDT_1h_1500d.pkl`). No new data, one script. That single test killed the covered call's headline (+44.3% CAGR became a dead heat from any 2023 or 2024 start); if the %R exposure result survives it, the archetype has its first genuine leg. Pass condition: time-in-market advantage holds with return within noise of buy-and-hold from **at least four of six start years**.

### 2. Premium harvesting on an existing spot position

**Mechanism.** You hold the coin anyway. Selling the coin's optionality converts an asset you were already exposed to into a yield stream, and — critically — needs **no purchased wing**, which is what kills every defined-risk variant (wings cost **43% of ATM premium**; every capped variant lands at ratio **−0.257 to −0.385**).

**Components and numbers.**
- Covered call, 285 non-overlapping weeks, 2021-03 to 2026-09: **CC ATM +44.3% CAGR / maxDD −32.9% / Sharpe 1.34** vs **spot +7.7% / −75.9% / 0.41**.
- **Half the headline is compounding, not the trade**: the fixed-1-BTC control gives **+21.6% CAGR**, not +44.3%. Report the control figure if coin count is meant to stay fixed.
- **Start-date sensitivity is the governing result**: from a 2023 start the overlay **loses** (+51.4% vs +53.4%); from 2024 it ties (+24.9% vs +24.2%). The entire 5.5-year edge lives in 2021–2022, when the sample began near a cycle top. Trap #7 (missing drift control) applies with force.
- **Robustness that does hold**: sign stable across all 7 disjoint weekly grids (magnitude swings ~35%); survives a **−4 vol-point execution haircut** (+28.7% vs spot +7.7%).
- **Parity correction, and it matters.** The covered call at K *is* the cash-secured put at K — verified week-by-week to machine precision (max weekly divergence 2.22e-16 at ATM). So the structure's P&L stream is the **put leg (+0.810%/wk, t = +3.12, positive in 5 of 6 years)**, not the call leg. The call leg (+0.378%, t=+1.28) is only the *difference versus a 100%-BTC benchmark*. Correspondingly, "sell cash-secured puts instead" is **not a new idea to test — it is the same position**, already computed twice (+0.810 vs +0.809).
- **And the leg decomposition is an identity, not a finding.** `put − call = spot return` exactly (measured residual 0.000000000000 over all 285 trades). "The put leg carries 68% of the P&L for 50% of the premium" restates "BTC returned +0.430%/wk", and that drift has **t = +0.96**. Repricing off the futures forward (live F/S = **+0.0923%**; funding-implied 7d forward premium over the sample **+0.161%**) moves call to +0.464% (t +1.57) and put to +0.733% (t +2.82), and reduces the gap to **63% of the drift**.
- **Risk is capital, not forgone upside.** A short put's downside is undiminished, merely cushioned by the ~3.3% premium — see the structure's own **maxDD −32.9%**, **worst week −23.2%**, and **CC +10% at −45.0% in 2022**.
- **The gate that must sit in front of any of it** (STRATEGY_SHORT_VOL.md): `margin = hist_win − need_win ≥ +5pp`, priced with **bid for legs sold, ask for legs bought**. Live output 2026-09-09: all four candidates failed (**−3.9 / −1.0 / −4.3 / −28.6 pp**). Also binding: never price anything under ~5 days off DVOL (1d ATM IV = **0.78×** DVOL, 7d = **1.01×**); the 1-day edge falls from +0.514% to ~**+0.20%** once corrected, and 2026 goes negative.
- **The premium is dormant, not competed away**: no significant decay (**t = −1.26**; trend slope **t = −1.50**); the IV/RV ratio is intact at **92% of its 2021 value** while the IV level halved to **47.6%**. It scales back up with vol. There is no entry timing available from DVOL level: quartiles give **+3.45 / +1.14 / +1.73 / +3.01** vol points — no monotonicity.
- **Funding carry is the same family and validates ~14× faster**: **+3.4%/yr** untimed, **+7.7%** holding only the top funding quartile, funding positive **76.6%** of prints, lag-1 autocorrelation **+0.75**. Costs: **~0.20% round trip = 21 days of carry** at 3.4%/yr, and **20% of rolling 30-day windows were negative** (median +4.1%, range −3.6% to +7.6%).

**Evidenced vs hypothesis.** *Evidenced that the structure exists and has a mechanism; not evidenced that it beats holding the coin.* The vol premium's existence is the best-replicated positive result in the repo. The covered-call overlay's **alpha over spot is not established** — arithmetic t < 2 everywhere, geometric t ≈ 2 but ≈ 0.08 Bonferroni-corrected, and the start-date grid removes the headline entirely. Funding carry is the one component whose edge/noise ratio (**+0.72 to +0.78, 3× anything else in the repo**) makes it verifiable in weeks.

**Cheapest test.** Two, in order of cost:
1. **Free, today:** re-run the existing 285-week series with **Black-76 forward pricing on the causal trailing-7d basis and a real strike-level smile**, then re-run the **start-date grid on the corrected series**. All inputs cached (`dvol_btc_2200d.pkl`, `spot_BTCUSDT_1d_2200d.pkl`). Pass condition: overlay beats spot from ≥4 of 6 start years after correction. The reviewer's partial repricing suggests this *helps* (CC ATM +44.3% → +50.9%; CC +10% +18.7% → +31.3%), which is exactly why it must be done properly rather than assumed.
2. **Cheap, forward:** run the funding-carry quartile rule live at minimum size. Its n-for-t=2 is **7–8 trades** — it either confirms or dies inside a quarter, and it is the only component in the catalogue with that property. The open question the catalogue does *not* answer is whether quartile toggling's extra yield survives paying a full 0.20% round trip per toggle; measure toggle frequency first.

### 3. Filters and vetoes — removing trades rather than generating them

**Mechanism.** A rule with negative expectancy is as useful as one with positive expectancy, provided you already have a trade population to subtract from. This inverts the multiple-comparisons problem: a *prohibition* that replicates on independent samples does not need to beat a fee, because it is not a trade.

**Components — this is the most replicated family in the repo.**
- **Prohibition 1:** do not buy sweep-reclaims in BEAR — **−0.095R (n=796, t=−2.54)** and **−0.209R (n=186, t=−2.92)**. Same sign, both samples.
- **Prohibition 2:** do not sell sweep-rejects in BULL — **−0.102R (n=782, t=−2.75)** and **−0.149R (n=197, t=−2.15)**.
- **Prohibition 3:** do not fade a range boundary at any timeframe — **24 of 24 daily configurations negative**, worst **−5.17% (t=−4.24, win 26%)** against a random-entry control of only −0.13%; intraday, fade + follow = **−0.100% = exactly 2× the 0.05% fee**, i.e. the boundary carries zero directional information to three decimals.
- **Fourth independent replication:** %R **follow beats fade at every length × hold cell** tested on 3,200 daily bars.
- **Applied as a filter, it pays:** the **EMA150 gate** on S-003 drops **9 losers and 1 winner** out of sample, worth **+800pp** net, taking 2018–2026 from ~+1551% to ~+2370%. The **EMA-cross 20/100 veto** takes Sharpe 1.00 → 1.07 and maxDD **−59% → −41%** while binding **~1% of days**, and sits on a **plateau** (five fast/slow pairs all land Sharpe 1.04–1.08, maxDD −37…−43%) rather than a tuned point.
- **What the veto beats:** a 50/50 capital split between the two engines is worse (**maxDD −55…−69%**); Donchian as the partner engine is worse; trading RSI(3) mean-reversion during the chop instead of staying flat is **−8% CAGR**.

**Evidenced vs hypothesis.** *The prohibitions are evidenced — four independent replications of the same polarity across 5m, 15m, daily and oscillator formulations. Their transferability is a hypothesis.* The EMA150 and EMA-cross gates are only measured **on S-003, on the sample they were selected against**, and the review flags **v3/unified out-of-sample contamination** (2024–2026 reused for further selection). Three further cautions belong in any use: the **signal-overlap rule** (daily ADX and weekly EMA5/21 are transforms of the same price history — do not multiply their success rates as independent probabilities); the S-003 headline Sharpe of 10.45 is **breadth-inflated 11.5×**, with honest per-trade **IR 0.91 [0.51, 1.51] on ~4 trades/yr**; and the backtest's compounding-from-2011 equity path implies buying ~3.12 million BTC at $0.32 — capacity is unmodelled.

**Cheapest test.** Apply the EMA150 / EMA-cross veto to a trade population it was **never fitted on** — the 264 %R swing trades, or the daily-consolidation-breakout signals (18.5/yr) — and measure maxDD and return separately. Everything needed is cached. Pass condition: maxDD falls materially with return inside noise, on a population the veto's parameters never saw. Fail condition (equally informative): the veto's benefit does not survive leaving S-003, in which case it is a property of that one strategy's failure mode and should be documented as such rather than promoted to a portfolio rule.

### 4. Risk sizing — measurable but non-predictive quantities used to scale, not to time

**Mechanism.** Several quantities in the catalogue reliably measure *how dangerous the next few hours are* without saying anything about direction. Direction-free risk information is worthless for entries and potentially valuable for size, stop placement and wing width. Crucially, this archetype is *immune to the fee wall* that killed the prediction studies: changing size costs nothing.

**Components with real numbers.**
- **Liquidation-density map**: P(cascade within 2h) by density quintile **3.58 / 3.69 / 4.23 / 4.67 / 5.17%** against a base rate of **4.27%** — cleanly monotonic, **z = −6.9 … +9.1** over 209,807 scored bars, and it **survives the visit-count confound** at every stratum (+0.89 / +1.14 / +1.17 pp, z +5.9 / +7.5 / +7.7).
- **24h rolling extreme**: the 24h low is the nearest level at **27.0%** of cascades vs **8.2%** of random bars (**3.31×**); the 24h high **18.5% vs 9.8% (1.89×)**. Cascades sit a median **0.140%** from the nearest stop level vs **0.306%** for random bars (**t = −3.71**).
- **Cascade base rate**: **17.5/month**, median magnitude **0.69%**, p90 **1.27%**. The proxy is validated at **40× enrichment (z = +69.9)** against 53,398 real forced liquidations.
- **Volatility is forecastable even though price is not**: five independent estimators converge at **43 / 43 / 45 / 45 / 47% → ~45% annualised**; GARCH persistence **0.94**, ν **4.65**. And the one calibration fix in the repo with `tradeable` status: **vol × 1.2 plus trailing drift** takes 90%-interval coverage from **0.75–0.82 to 0.88–0.93** and PIT from **0.34 to 0.49**.
- **The noise floor any stop must clear**: post-cascade median **1.6% travel with 1.3% adverse excursion** over the following 24h; sweep-reclaim **stop-out rate 54%** on a ~0.4% median stop; MAE/MFE symmetric at every horizon (**60m −0.38/+0.39, 120m −0.51/+0.54, 240m −0.68/+0.69**).
- **Existing sizing rule to plug into**: max loss ≤ **1.5% of stack** per position, ≤1 open position, ≤**6% of stack** cumulative max-loss exposure across all option positions.

**What is explicitly *not* supported.** Density predicts **initiation, not termination**: it does not predict cascade magnitude (0.686% dense vs 0.661% sparse), does not mark where moves stop (t = +1.85), travel is uncorrelated with cluster distance (**corr −0.043**), and the direct monetisation test — resting bids in high-density buckets — is **negative in all four configurations (−0.012% to −0.106%)**. As a directional feature, `liq_dens` scores **−0.0048%, t = −1.33**, is the most independent feature in the matrix (**max |corr| 0.02**), and appears in **zero** top combinations. That combination — genuinely independent *and* completely non-predictive — is the repo's structural finding in miniature.

**Evidenced vs hypothesis.** *The risk measurements are evidenced; every sizing application is an untested hypothesis, and the catalogue says so in three separate places* (LIQUIDATION_FINDINGS §7.1 stop displacement, §7.2 density-conditioned routing, §7.5 cascade probability as a short-vol sizing overlay). **No sizing benefit has been measured anywhere in this programme.** It is still worth stating because it is the only archetype where the repo's strongest non-predictive statistics (3.31×, 1.21× monotonic, 40× proxy validation) have a use that does not require them to beat a fee.

**Cheapest test.** Take an existing trade population that already carries stops — the **1,156 long + 1,102 short sweep setups**, cached 5m bars and cascade map both on disk — and change **only the stop placement**: displace it away from the 24h rolling extreme and out of high-density buckets, holding the risk budget constant. Measure stop-out rate against the **54% baseline** and R-multiple against the existing table. This costs one script and zero new data. Pass condition: stop-out rate falls materially at equal risk budget. If it does not, §7.1 dies cleanly and the density map is demoted to descriptive.

### 5. Execution-layer edges — the actual arithmetic of a better fee tier

**Mechanism.** Fees are not a friction term in this repo, they are the binding constraint: seventeen studies located real effects and lost all of them to cost. Fee reduction is the only single change that moves every intraday table at once.

**The arithmetic, corrected.** The naive version ("maker is free") is wrong and the catalogue corrects it: Binance USDT-M **maker is 0.02%/side, so maker-maker is a real 4bp round trip**, not zero. At **5% sigma capture** (the observed clustering), the minimum viable holding period by tier is:

| execution tier | round trip | min viable hold @5% capture |
|---|---|---|
| taker / taker | 10 bp | 2.9 days |
| maker / taker | 7 bp | 33.9 h |
| maker / maker | 4 bp | 11.1 h |
| maker / maker @8% capture (optimistic) | 4 bp | 4.3 h |

Against a 4bp floor, the measured directional edges are: **ofi5 @5min 0.50bp**, **best 3-signal combination @1h 2.5bp**, **tint5 @15min 1.80bp**, **book imbalance @60min 3.6bp**, **post-cascade reversion 10.9bp gross**. Only the last clears 4bp with room. The sweep-setup fee curve gives the sensitivity directly — **CHOP long: 0.00% → +0.170R, 0.05% → +0.022R, 0.10% → −0.127R**, a slope of about **−3R per 1% of round-trip cost** — and the stop-size ratio explains why: a 0.10% fee is **0.25R on a ~0.4% median stop**.

**The trap that makes this archetype smaller than it looks.** The maker version of the one surviving candidate **has already been run and it lost.** Resting bids — passive execution, exactly the maker case — score **−0.102% LONG / −0.099% SHORT unblocked**, and **−0.068% (t=−1.97) / −0.094% (t=−2.99)** on a non-overlapping subsample. Passive fills into a cascade are adversely selected precisely because that is where forced flow is. Separately, no fee reduction can rescue a rule whose **out-of-sample sign is negative** — and the sweep setups' best long config tests at **−0.174R (t=−2.67)**. Cheaper execution multiplies a positive OOS edge; it does nothing to a zero or negative one.

**Evidenced vs hypothesis.** *The cost arithmetic is evidenced and is one of the more reliable things in the catalogue. The claim that any specific edge becomes tradeable at 4bp is a hypothesis, and the one direct test of it (resting bids) failed.* Maker-only execution carries `untested` status precisely because nobody has built a fill model.

**Cheapest test.** Not a backtest — a **fill-and-adverse-selection measurement**. Post passive quotes at minimum size for a fixed window and record: (a) fill rate, (b) realised slippage versus the quoted price, (c) forward return conditional on being filled. (c) is the whole question: if fills cluster before continuation, the 4bp saving is spent immediately and the tier upgrade is worth nothing. The kill criterion already exists in the strategy doc — **slippage above ~10% of credit means execution is eating the edge**, above 15% on 5 consecutive fills is a stop.

### 6. Cross-sectional / relative value — genuinely untested, and why the prior differs

**Mechanism.** Every failed study in this programme predicted the *level* of one series. A spread trades the *relationship* between two, which changes the problem in three ways: the spread is typically closer to stationary than either leg; the natural frequency is higher; and some spreads are not predictions at all but **premium differentials**, which puts them in the family that has 20–80% sigma capture rather than the family with 3–13%.

**What is on the list, and what is actually known about each.**
- **ETH/BTC relative value.** Status `untested`, **no numbers whatsoever** in the catalogue. Both legs are on cached Binance klines.
- **Option term-structure / calendar spread.** The *level* differential is measured on a single live chain (2026-09-09): **1d ATM IV = 0.78× DVOL, 1.6d = 0.91×, 8.6d = 1.01×, 15.6d = 0.96×, 50.6d = 0.95×**. The *persistence* of that shape is unmeasured — **n = 1 day**. This is the most interesting item on the list because it falls directly out of trap #4 and it is a premium trade, not a direction trade.
- **Forward-collected order-book imbalance.** Status `untested`; Binance serves no historical depth, so it requires ~1 month of polling to build a dataset. The related question of **resting-order stacking at levels** is closed on public history entirely (bookTicker discontinued ~2024-03).
- **The 2025-10-06 ATH spot-sell window.** Not a spread, but the same category of "not yet actually tested": the existing spot-distribution study ran on a 90-day sample that **rose +27.9% with no drawdown worse than −5.99%** — the hypothesis was not refuted, it was tested where it could not fire.

**Why the prior legitimately differs from outright direction — and where it does not.** The reason to expect more from spreads is the **hold-length scaling law**: drift/carry edges grow with T while noise grows with √T (ratio ~√T, hold longer), whereas one-shot prediction edges are constant against √T noise (ratio ~1/√T). Every failed prediction study was one-shot. A carry-like or premium-like spread sits on the favourable side of that law. But the counter-prior is equally sharp: **independence-xor-prediction** — everything in this market that predicts is a rearrangement of one order flow (the predictive cluster correlates 0.53–0.57), and everything genuinely independent predicts nothing. A spread between two crypto legs may simply be one order flow again. And the cross-asset version is already dead: BTC/equity contemporaneous correlation is **0.37 intraday / 0.44–0.48 daily with zero lead** (equity→BTC Granger F 0.34–1.90 against critical values 6.63/3.78/2.80), and BTC leads equities rather than the reverse (**F 14.62 / 5.59 / 6.86, all significant — but R² gain +0.285pp**). There is no reaction window between them, so a BTC-vs-equity spread has nothing to trade.

**Evidenced vs hypothesis.** **Speculation, explicitly. No cross-sectional relative-value effect has been measured in this repo at all.** It is still worth stating for one reason: it is the last untested domain that does not inherit the fee-wall failure mode by construction, and stating it plainly is what stops it being quietly assumed to work.

**Cheapest test — and this one is nearly free.** Do **not** backtest either idea. Run the **two-axis screen** first: compute edge and per-trade noise on cached data, get `n for t=2 = (2·noise/edge)²`, and reject before any compute if **ratio < 0.05 and trades/yr < 1000** (unlearnable) or **edge < 2× round-trip fee** (unprofitable). Applied retrospectively this screen rejects six of ten prior studies before a single backtest. For the calendar spread the equivalent minimum test is a **daily poll of per-expiry ATM IV from the Deribit public chain for 30 days** — no backtest, no history required — to establish whether the 0.78× / 1.01× shape is a standing feature or a single draw.

### Composing them — and the double-counting trap

These archetypes are not additive, and the catalogue names the reason twice: **price-derived transforms are not independent votes** (FVGs, profiles, moving averages, ADX and EMA crosses are all functions of the same OHLC), and the **signal-overlap rule** forbids multiplying their historical success rates. Two concrete overlaps to watch:

- The **covered call (archetype 2)** and **funding carry (2)** are both short-vol / long-drift in disguise, and both are net-long the same cycle. Adding them is adding leverage to one position, not diversifying.
- The **exposure-management (1)** and **veto (3)** archetypes both reduce time in market from the same price series. Running both is not two risk reductions.

Independence stacking has already been tested here and it failed on its own terms: three independent 52% signals predicted a ~56–57% combined win rate; the best achieved across 120 combinations was **53.8% / 2.5bp**, with every combination underperforming the independence prediction and the best combination's train t (**+0.77**) *below* the best single feature's (**+2.05**).

The honest ordering that falls out of all of it: **funding carry** first (validates in 7–8 trades), **the prohibitions/vetoes** second (already replicated four ways, and they cost nothing to apply), **risk sizing** third (measurements are solid, the application is one cheap script away from an answer), **premium harvesting** fourth (real mechanism, unresolved alpha, 40–100 weeks to validate), **execution** fifth (arithmetic is clear, the one direct test failed), **relative value** last (zero evidence, stated so it is not assumed).
