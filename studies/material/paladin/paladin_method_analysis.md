# Paladin: the method, the record, and the gap between them

*Discord channel, 2026-05-06 to 2026-08-21. 1,005 messages, 320 images, 217 reconstructed positions. Every quote below is verbatim and message-verified.*

---

## What he actually watches

The toolkit is the most surprising thing in the archive, because there is almost nothing in it. Across 1,005 messages he names exactly six technical inputs: the EMA (20, 50 and 200, usually on the 4h), the 4h candle close, support and resistance, the prior daily high and low, wicks, and round thousand-dollar levels on BTC. That is the complete list.

What is absent is easier to state. RSI, divergence, fibonacci, open interest, order flow, orderbook, CVD, delta, liquidation heatmaps, fair value gaps, order blocks, smart money, Wyckoff, Elliott, supply-demand zones, trendlines, stochastics, MACD, Bollinger bands and VWAP return zero occurrences of any kind. "Volume" appears four times and "funding" once — every instance inside the description of a screenshot he posted for some other reason, exchange interface visible behind a chart. He never writes either word himself and never reasons from either.

He does trade one concept he refuses to name. "Liquidity" never appears, but the sweep does, in plain language: "as I said they'll make a new daily low then bounce it, you can take 25-30%" (msg 589). He buys the flush, he just doesn't dress it up.

The absence extends to charts themselves. Of 320 attached images, 247 are PnL share cards and only 23 — seven percent — are charts, several posted after the move. He treats this as a feature: "Gave BTC prediction a week ago, no messy charts, no long explanation, just one line" (msg 736).

What remains is a level-and-calendar practice. On BTC he narrates price as a sequence of round numbers — "For now 64.1-64.2k is a wall, reclaim that and 65-66k in no time" (msg 815) — and builds entries, DCAs and targets on that grid. The 200 EMA is his one named indicator, converted straight into a price ("we shouldn't lose the 200 ema on 4h that is 77.7k", msg 131) and traded as a bounce zone: "this is how EMA 200 acts, if it's reclaims, it's very hard to breakdown, you can expect at least 2 bounce from EMA 200 on any time frame" (msg 968).

Around that sit non-chart inputs he weights heavily: the macro calendar (CPI, FOMC, monthly close), political headlines, a belief in weekend and Sunday pumps, the rule that BTC drives every alt position, relative strength in alts that refuse to fall during a BTC dump, and a mild contrarian read on the chat.

The 4h close needs one correction. It is his most-cited piece of chart logic in commentary — 35 messages — but only 14 of 139 signal posts publish a candle-close stop, and only 10 pair it with a wider hard stop. Nine signals in ten carry a plain numeric stop. It matters most when a trade is going against him, as the reason not to accept the loss yet.

He never explains where the levels come from; he states them as conclusions. That is the real gap in the record, and it sits exactly where the edge would be if there is one.

## How he structures a trade

The template is fixed and never varies:

> Long BTC
> Entry: 63960 CMP
> SL: 62.5k

Entry at market ("CMP" appears in 240 messages), because he believes resting limits get pre-empted: "Also a good price to enter is 65-65.1k but I don't want to take my chances front ran" (msg 711). Size is announced as a percentage split — 50/50, 40/40/20 — spelled out for anyone who cannot do it: "if you don't know how to divide easy way is to go to chatgpt and tell to divide the total amount into 40-40-20%" (msg 443).

Risk is fixed by rule, not by conviction: 1-2% on alts, 3-5% on BTC, sized off a position-size calculator rather than off leverage. "You don't have to worry how much leverage to use, you can use even 500x as long as you're writing down the same amount" (msg 218). His one active sizing lever is the "half risk" tag, applied in 54 messages to shitcoins, pumped markets, weekends, pre-event entries and trades against his own bias.

The planning discipline is measurable. Of the 200 positions he took, 180 (90%) shipped with a published stop, 118 (59%) with a published target, 57 (29%) with a published DCA level.

**A worked example: BTC long, 10-17 August.**

On 10 August at 19:23 he posted the entry above — 63,960 at market, stop 62.5k — two minutes after covering a short. Five minutes later he filled in the rest: "SL is 62.5k I'm just saying I think 61.5k we go for a bounce on HTF... From scalping point of view we can get 500-700$ bounce from this region, I usually target 0.5R for scalps... 25 DCA @ 63523 (optional)" (msg 929). Half margin across his two open longs. No target published.

That is the whole setup: a round-ish level, a wide stop 1,460 points below, an optional quarter-size add lower, and a size instruction. No chart, no indicator, no derivation.

On 12 August the DCA filled, taking the blended entry to 63,797: "I'm targeting 64.5-64.6k for now can pump upto 65, will exit when I see rejection... If anyone wants to exit before CPI they can safely" (msg 937). A target appeared for the first time two days after entry, framed as discretionary from the start.

It missed twice, reversing from 64,550 and then 64,450. On 14 August, with price falling back toward the stop, he changed the stop's nature rather than its price: "Keeping BTC SL manual for now, will wait for 4h candle below 62.5" (msg 944). The hard order came off; only a 4h close would now count.

It survived. On 17 August he cut the target — "Looking to close in small profits 64-64.2k, just wanna get out of this one" (msg 954) — and ninety minutes later closed at 64,144.9: "Closing BTC here in some profits. Profits aren't much but what's nice is we survived a SL, the win streak continues!" (msg 957), then "From almost an SL to a decent TP with a DCA in" (msg 959).

The share card read +109.01%. Measured properly — 348 points of gain against 1,297 points of risk — the trade was worth 0.27R, held seven days. It is a win in the record, and it is representative: entry planned, target invented mid-trade, stop softened under pressure, exit taken early and manually.

## How he manages a trade once live

Management is continuous and instruction-heavy. Every milestone produces a percentage: "Up 1.2R take 10-20% only, save MAJORITY!" (msg 326). Of 200 positions, 69 had their target changed and 45 had a stop moved. He teaches stop-to-entry after TP1 in roughly 44 messages, applies it to 22 positions, and announces when he is not: "Personally didn't move SL to entry, will play full setup either SL or TP" (msg 182).

The clearest contradiction in the archive is partials. He built an identity on refusing them — "Since I don't take partials and take only 1TP so calculating my real R is very easy!" (msg 802) — and the record contains 47 partial exits across 43 positions, sized from 10% to 75%. On 11 June at 15:59 he wrote "I'm still in BTC long from 62.2k I only took 25% at 63k" (msg 457). At 22:42 the same day: "Made 3.2R today, that's realized without any partials, I don't take partials!" (msg 465).

Exits are discretionary by design. He usually leaves no resting take-profit — "I take manual TPs but you can set in case" (msg 5) — and closes on a rejection, a wick, or nerves. When he is asleep he outsources it: "If you see wicks try to TP too many positions to update at once and it's my sleeping time" (msg 451).

Stops move against him rarely but decisively: fifteen widen, extend, remove or hard-to-manual events across 217 positions. The worst is unambiguous — "Removing BTC SL for now will wait a 4h close above 65.2k... Reason is if I get stopped I'll short again so not gonna accept my SL here" (msg 969) — and he once ran a stopless ETH short while inviting followers into it (msgs 853, 860). This is the sharpest risk in following him, and it is 7% of his trades, not 70%. Median hold is 17.2 hours.

## How he wins

Strip the narration and one fact does most of the work.

**Discretionary exits never lose.** Of 217 positions, 62 closed by manual exit — more than the 45 that reached a take-profit. Sixty-one are wins, one is breakeven, and none is a loss. Every one of his 35 losses arrived through a stop. The asymmetry is structural rather than skilful: he controls when to book and the market controls when he loses, so the act he controls only ever produces green.

**Most of his winners therefore never reached the plan.** Only 45 of 120 wins — 38% — closed at a target he had published; at least 26 were manual closes booked short of the stated TP. On 26 July he closed a BTC long at 64.8k against his own published 65.5k target, and BTC tagged 65,555 the following morning. A follower who set the published TP and waited would have had a materially different, and worse, experience than his tally implies.

**Targets are small and taken fast.** He is explicit — "Yes my R is small 0.5-0.8R on avg but there are so many TPs that 2 trades cover 1SL easily" (msg 802) — and the record only half-agrees. Of 120 wins, 27 carry a stated R; median 1.00R, mean 1.05R, eight at exactly 0.5R and two above 2R. That sample is self-selected, since he publishes R when it flatters. Either way the trade is the same: a high hit rate bought with a small multiple.

**He is long-biased in a market that recovered.** 152 longs against 47 shorts among the taken positions; longs won 78% of resolved outcomes, shorts 74%. His monthly win rate rises through the sample — 73%, 76%, 82%, 83% — tracking BTC's path from the high 70s down to the high 50s and back above where it started. His worst stretch came from shorting against his own view: "I'm the most bullish guy here and I'm getting fked in shorts" (msg 977), five stops in two days.

**Two things people assume are the engine are not.** The reserved DCA tranche is a rescue tool, not a win-rate machine: only 30 of 200 positions ever fill one, and those that do win 68% against 80% for the rest — unsurprising, since a DCA fills precisely when the entry was wrong. Soft and moveable stops are similarly overstated: 10% of signals carry a candle-close stop, 7% of positions ever have one widened, and all 35 losses still closed at a stop.

The rest is unglamorous and real: a fixed template, a stop on 90% of trades, sizing off a calculator, and the discipline to stop after a good run — "My weekly goal has been done, so I'll take it slow until next week" (msg 875).

## Where the scoreboard and the record diverge

Reconstructing every position he posted gives 217 catalogued setups, 200 actually taken, 165 with a knowable outcome: **120 wins, 35 losses, 10 breakevens — 77% excluding breakevens, 73% including them.**

His own summaries claim more.

| | He claimed | Reconstructed | Gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| **May** | 60 trades, 51W, 9L, **85.0%** | 54 taken, 50 resolved: 36W, 13L, 1BE = **73%** | 12 points; 13 losses found against 9 claimed |
| **June** | *No summary was ever posted* | 63 taken (his busiest month), 49 resolved: 34W, 11L, 4BE = **76%** | Unmeasurable — and it is the month he promised to count |
| **July** | 45 trades, 40W, 5 SLs, **89%**, 93% BTC | 46 taken, 41 resolved: 31W, 7L, 3BE = **82%**; BTC 13W/2L = **87%** | 7 points; trade counts match almost exactly, outcomes do not |

Four things explain most of it.

The breakeven bucket disappeared. His pre-server journal recap reported three categories — "**25** trades... **22** Wins... **3** BE... **0** loss" (msg 2). Every summary posted once he had an audience reports only Trades, Wins and Losses. With ten breakevens in the record, that change alone moves the headline several points.

Ambiguous outcomes are resolved by him, occasionally by referendum: "Just checking because I don't know if I should count it as a win or loss as it went to huge profits, I'll pick what the majority says please be honest!" (msg 403).

Whole categories are declared unofficial before the fact and therefore cannot lose — gold and oil ("I won't count gold trades in crypto summary", msg 405), 5m entries ("Just an idea won't count TP or SL, not an official trade", msg 135), coins he posts but does not take, late-month setups. And not every trade reaches the channel: "I post all my trades in this server but since I wanna grow my X too some of my personal or once in a while trades will be there" (msg 877).

None of this requires bad faith, and none of it supports a fraud reading. A reconstructed 77% win rate over 200 positions is a real and good result. The finding is narrower: the trading is better than average, and the bookkeeping is informal, generous, and generous in the same direction every time.

## What is replicable, and what is not

**Replicable.** The template — a price, a stop, a size, all published before the entry — is the most valuable thing here and the least glamorous. So is sizing from a risk calculator rather than from a leverage number, keeping one adjustable lever ("half risk") instead of many, capping concurrent positions, and stopping once a periodic target is met. So, in principle, is the core trade: small targets on round levels in a liquid instrument, held under a day. That combination genuinely does produce a high hit rate, and he is honest that it costs him R.

**Not replicable.** The level selection, first — he never shows the derivation, and it is the input that would matter most. Then the exits: his win rate depends on being awake, watching, and willing to book below plan, which no one copying a signal feed can reproduce, and setting his published TP captures 38% of his winners. Then the accounting: comparing yourself to 85-90% is comparing yourself to a number that was never audited. And finally the stop discipline — copying msg 969, where the stop comes off because "if I get stopped I'll short again", is how accounts end.

The R arithmetic is worth internalising separately: his +109.01% share card on the August BTC trade was 0.27R. Leverage inflates the card and changes nothing about the trade.

## What the data cannot tell us

There is no account size anywhere in the record. The only balances ever shown are the $89.36 novelty account he told members not to copy and a four-day +30.92% curve. Position sizes in dollars are never posted, and every ROI figure is a leveraged exchange card (median 179.7%, modal leverage 150x), not an account return.

Everything is self-reported: no exchange statements, no verified equity curve, and three primary venues in four months — Bybit, then Blofin, then Yubit — with the newest the most obscure. Thirty-five of the 200 taken positions have no recoverable outcome; the reconstruction excludes them rather than guessing, which if anything understates both his wins and his losses. Some trades never appear in the channel at all, and a VIP tier is referenced but not visible.

And the sample stops at the worst possible moment for judging it: five stops in two days, an apology — "5 SLs is how much u have monthly usually" (msg 997) — and a final action of "Closing long at BE, getting risky and I already have 5 SLs I'm gonna play safe" (msg 1005). Whatever the true win rate is, it was measured entirely inside a market that went up, and the method has never been observed through a losing month.
