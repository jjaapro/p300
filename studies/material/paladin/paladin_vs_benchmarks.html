How the estimate is built

Net R × risk per trade. Both inputs come from his own numbers.

Why it cannot be tighter than that

Four gaps in the source, each of which widens the band

No account size, ever

Not once in 1,005 messages does he state what he trades with. Only 10 positions carry a dollar PnL, and 8 of those belong to a disclosed $89 novelty account.

Card ROI is not account return

A "+331.94%" share card at 150× leverage is a 2.2% move in the underlying on whatever margin was posted. Those headline percentages say nothing about the account.

Several accounts at once

The same idea appears as a Blofin 150× card, a MEXC 250× card and an $89 challenge-account fill. One trade, three numbers, no way to weight them.

The record is self-selected

35 positions are never resolved in the channel, and he concedes some trades go only to X and never to the server. Unposted trades cannot be assumed to resemble posted ones.

There is also a ceiling the arithmetic hides. Compounding 4% risk across 155 trades at his win rate gives roughly 45× over four months — a number nobody achieves. Fees, funding, slippage, position sizes that do not scale with equity, and the fact that he often runs three to five correlated positions at once all eat it. The table above therefore uses simple R × risk, not compounding, which is the conservative choice.

What the comparison leaves out

Return is only half of a fair benchmark

None of this is risk-adjusted, and the risk is not comparable. Holding the S&P through this window meant a quiet 5%. Holding bitcoin meant sitting through a 28% drawdown to end 7% down. His book carried 75× to 250× leverage on a stop that was typically 1–4% away, several positions at a time, through the same drawdown — a stop honoured late on a 150× position can end an account in one candle, and the record shows him widening or removing a stop 15 times.

The four-month window also flatters everything about his approach. He is structurally long — 152 longs against 47 shorts — in a market that fell and then recovered past its starting point. His worst stretch, five stops in two days in August, came from shorting against his own bullish view. A directional book with small targets does well when the direction is right; the same book is what produced the losses.

One last thing the record shows plainly: his own spot bitcoin, bought at about $63,500 on 11 August, was up 20.8% by the 21st. He timed the buy far better than a passive holder from May — which is the part of his edge that survives every caveat above.
